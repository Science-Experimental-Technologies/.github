## About Us

Science Experimental Technologies (SCIX) is an independent organization exploring the intersection of science, engineering, computing, and emerging technologies. We build open software for practical problems—from connecting AI models behind one interface to making astronomical analysis more reproducible. Our work matters when complex tools and scientific methods need to become inspectable, reusable, and easier to evaluate. We publish our projects in the open and make their scope and limitations visible.

## Mission & Vision

**Mission** — Build and share useful technology that helps people investigate hard problems with rigor and openness.

**Vision** — A future where research-grade ideas can be examined, adapted, and put to work by a wider community.

### How we approach the work

- Start with a question that can be stated and evaluated.
- Prefer small, inspectable components over opaque claims.
- Record assumptions, data sources, and known limitations.
- Invite review from people with relevant technical or scientific context.
- Treat user feedback as input to prioritization, not as a promise of delivery.

## Why Partner With Us

We work in public, so potential collaborators can inspect source, decisions, and project-specific license terms before proposing an adoption. Our portfolio pairs software engineering with applied research, and we aim to make methods and limitations clear enough for informed review. Integration scope, support expectations, and security requirements should be agreed for each engagement; they are not blanket guarantees.

| For | Collaboration value |
| --- | --- |
| **Companies** | Evaluate an open foundation, discuss a scoped integration, or co-design a pilot against a concrete use case. |
| **Researchers** | Review reproducible workflows, propose validation work, or explore a joint research question. |
| **Contributors** | Improve software, documentation, tests, and research artifacts through a public contribution process. |

## Get Involved

1. **Choose a project** and read its README, license, and contribution notes.
2. **Find a task** in [good first issues](https://github.com/issues?q=org%3AScience-Experimental-Technologies+is%3Aissue+is%3Aopen+label%3A%22good+first+issue%22) or open an issue to discuss an idea.
3. **Send a focused pull request** with context, evidence, and any relevant tests or documentation updates.

Every repository may have its own setup and review requirements. Start with the project you intend to change.

### Before opening a pull request

- Check open issues and existing discussions for related work.
- Explain the problem and the behavior your change is intended to improve.
- Keep changes focused and include reproducible evidence when relevant.
- Update user-facing documentation when commands, outputs, or assumptions change.
- Avoid committing credentials, private data, or material without redistribution rights.

Maintainers may ask for revisions or additional evidence before merging. Scientific changes should make their assumptions and validation path clear to reviewers.
